# IELTS Speaking Mock — Health & Sleep

**Format:** Savol → sizning original javobingiz → Band 9 uslubidagi namunaviy javob → aynan namunada ishlatilgan collocationlar va o‘zbekcha tarjimasi.

**Eslatma:** Namunalar yuqori darajadagi tabiiy nutqni ko‘rsatadi; tayyor matnni yodlash Band 9 kafolati emas. Sizning original javoblaringiz xatolari bilan birga saqlangan. Oldingi matnli mock uchun taxminiy umumiy baho 5.5 edi; audio bo‘lmagani sababli talaffuz baholanmagan. Partlar uchun berilgan alohida raqamlar rasmiy IELTS ballari emas.

## Part 1 — Sleep & Health

### 1. How many hours do you usually sleep at night?

**Sizning original javobingiz**

> I usually sleep eight or seven hours during days, but it is mostly related when I begin or when I go to the bed for sleep. If I sleep at 12 p.m., I think I will wake up five and get up six. But I usually sleep at 10 p.m. and I wake up four or five, and this is enough for me.

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer**

I normally **get a solid seven hours** of sleep, although it depends on my schedule. I try to **wind down** at about ten and **stick to a routine** during the week. When I do that, I tend to **wake up feeling refreshed**. If I stay up unusually late, I may sleep a little longer the next morning to **make up for lost sleep**.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **get a solid seven hours** — yetti soat to‘laqonli uxlash
2. **wind down** — uyqu oldidan tinchlanish
3. **stick to a routine** — tartibga qat’iy amal qilish
4. **wake up feeling refreshed** — tetik holda uyg‘onish
5. **make up for lost sleep** — yetishmagan uyquning o‘rnini to‘ldirish

### 2. Do you ever have trouble falling asleep?

**Sizning original javobingiz**

> Yes, of course. I often trouble falling asleep when I have a lot of work, or if I use my phone or my notebook or my laptop at night, I can't get rid of it, and I usually keep doing it, using, keep doing using it, and ultimately if I didn't sleep at 10 p.m., I usually couldn't. I usually don't sleep till 1 a.m. or 2 a.m. And that's it.

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer**

Occasionally, especially when my **mind is racing** after a busy day. I can **have trouble drifting off** if I start checking messages in bed, because one notification leads to another. These days, I **put my phone away** and try to **stick to a bedtime**. A few quiet minutes with a book usually help me **fall asleep naturally**.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **mind is racing** — fikrlar tinmay aylanayotgan bo‘lishi
2. **have trouble drifting off** — uxlab qolishga qiynalish
3. **put my phone away** — telefonimni chetga qo‘yish
4. **stick to a bedtime** — belgilangan uxlash vaqtiga amal qilish
5. **fall asleep naturally** — tabiiy ravishda uxlab qolish

### 3. How do you feel the next day when you haven’t had enough sleep?

**Sizning original javobingiz**

> Oh my God. I usually, if I didn't sleep enough, next day I feel tired and anxious. But usually I usually drink water in the morning for relaxed and... it helps me. It helps me get rid of tired and better, and so that's it.

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer**

If I’m **running on little sleep**, I usually **feel mentally foggy** the next morning. I **struggle to concentrate**, even on fairly straightforward tasks, and I’m more impatient than usual. I try to **take a short break** when I can and avoid overloading my schedule. Then I aim for an earlier night so I can **catch up on sleep**.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **running on little sleep** — juda kam uyqu bilan kunni o‘tkazayotgan bo‘lish
2. **feel mentally foggy** — fikrni jamlash qiyin, xira his qilish
3. **struggle to concentrate** — diqqatni jamlashga qiynalish
4. **take a short break** — qisqa tanaffus qilish
5. **catch up on sleep** — yetishmagan uyquni qoplash

### 4. What do you do to stay healthy?

**Sizning original javobingiz**

> I usually do the small habits to stay healthy. For example, I always drink water in the morning and I just walk 50 or 20 minutes, and after that I usually eat healthy foods, like. And I usually, and I didn't eat, I don't eat a lot of sugar. Whenever I do regular cardio exercise, and they help me feel better and to stay healthy.

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer**

I focus on habits I can maintain. I try to **stay physically active** by taking the stairs and occasionally **go for a brisk walk** after studying. I also aim to **eat a balanced diet** rather than follow strict rules, and I’ve gradually **cut back on sugar**. Nothing is especially dramatic, but these small choices help me **keep my energy levels up**.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **stay physically active** — jismonan faol bo‘lib qolish
2. **go for a brisk walk** — tez qadamda sayrga chiqish
3. **eat a balanced diet** — muvozanatli ovqatlanish
4. **cut back on sugar** — shakar iste’molini kamaytirish
5. **keep my energy levels up** — quvvatni yuqori darajada saqlash

### 5. Have your health habits changed in recent years?

**Sizning original javobingiz**

> Absolutely yes. In recent years, I changed my a lot of health habits. For example, around five years ago, I usually played video games, and I spent a lot of time for social media, like Instagram or Telegram. But now I don't use them. I usually use it when I need it importantly. And now I prefer text or call messages, like. And I don't use my phone before 20 or 30 minutes before the bed. It helps me better sleep and calm.

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer**

Yes, quite a bit. I used to spend hours on my phone, but I’ve **cut down on screen time** and tried to **build a healthier routine**. It took a while to **break the habit of** checking social media whenever I had a spare minute. Now I **make a conscious effort** to put my phone aside before bed, and I generally **sleep more soundly** as a result.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **cut down on screen time** — ekran oldida o‘tkaziladigan vaqtni kamaytirish
2. **build a healthier routine** — sog‘lomroq tartib yaratish
3. **break the habit of** — bir odatni tashlash
4. **make a conscious effort** — ongli ravishda harakat qilish
5. **sleep more soundly** — tinchroq va chuqurroq uxlash

## Part 2 — Long turn

### Cue card: Describe a change you made that helped you sleep better.

You should say what you changed, when you made the change, why you decided to make it, and how it affected your sleep.

**Sizning original javobingiz**

> Today I will say my small habits, what they changed my fully life. For example, my daily routine, my sleep time, my wake up time, and for example, what helped me change my sleep time. Around two years ago, one year ago, I usually sleep at 12 p.m. or 1 a.m. It's midnight. So, and I wake up around 7 or 6 a.m. and I felt bad, and I couldn't sleep better and enough. So I tired. I felt tired during day. But around two months ago, I sit up. I changed my sleep time, and I go to bed at around 9 p.m, and I started reading books. And I didn't use my phone before 30 minutes before bed. It helps me better and stay calm before sleep. And I sleep at 10 p.m. or 10 past 30 minutes, and I wake up. I usually wake up at 4 or 5 a.m. It helps me to do more things in the morning, and when I wake up, I immediately drink one glass of water. It helps me better and, like, maybe not tired. And why I decided it, because before this decision, my work, I made a lot of mistakes in my work, and I couldn't study effectively, and my time also so effectless, and it looked like less than others. And I started and I decided to this decision, and it helped me more, give me more time and more chance. And whatever before the sleep, I usually do cardio for reduce stress. So, that's it. And it affected effectively sleep and easily wake up.

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer — taxminan 2 daqiqa**

I’d like to describe a small but surprisingly effective change to my evening routine. Until a couple of months ago, I had **fallen into a bad habit** of **staying up past midnight**. I would tell myself I was only going to check one message, but I often ended up **scrolling through social media** for an hour. Because I still needed to get up early for my studies, the lack of sleep began to **take a toll on my concentration**. I felt sluggish in the morning and sometimes had to reread the same page several times.

Eventually, I **made a conscious decision** to change that pattern. I began to **set a regular bedtime**, usually around ten o’clock, and **put my phone out of reach** about half an hour beforehand. Instead of staring at a screen, I would **wind down with a book**. I chose something enjoyable rather than difficult study material, so reading actually felt like a break. At first, I was tempted to check my phone, but after a week or two it became easier to **stick with the change**.

The biggest difference is how I feel in the morning. I don’t claim that every night is perfect, but I’m much more likely to **wake up feeling refreshed** and begin studying without that heavy, distracted feeling. I also have a little quiet time to plan my day. What I like about this change is that it didn’t require a complicated system: I simply replaced one unhelpful habit with a calmer one. I hope to keep doing it even if my timetable changes when I study abroad.

**Ishlatilgan 10 ta collocation**

1. **fallen into a bad habit** — yomon odatga tushib qolgan
2. **staying up past midnight** — yarim tundan keyin ham uyg‘oq yurish
3. **scrolling through social media** — ijtimoiy tarmoqlarni varaqlash
4. **take a toll on my concentration** — diqqatimga salbiy ta’sir qilish
5. **made a conscious decision** — ongli qaror qabul qildim
6. **set a regular bedtime** — muntazam uxlash vaqtini belgilash
7. **put my phone out of reach** — telefonimni qo‘l yetmaydigan joyga qo‘yish
8. **wind down with a book** — kitob o‘qib tinchlanish
9. **stick with the change** — o‘zgarishga sodiq qolish
10. **wake up feeling refreshed** — tetik holda uyg‘onish

## Part 3 — Sleep, technology and public health

### 1. Why do you think many people get less sleep nowadays?

**Sizning original javobingiz**

> In my opinion, people sleep less now than past because they work so bad, and in my opinion they try hard work or hard study, and I think they think sleep is not important, as if they are sleep, they just wasting of their time. But in my opinion it's so, like, stupid stuff, because sleep is the most important thing in our routine. Because sleep helps us to rest and relax and more effectively to do something, or more effectively to learning something. So I think they are tough, because this action, this situation.

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer — taxminan 35–40 soniya**

I think **long working hours** are part of the explanation, but technology also plays a role. With **constant digital stimulation**, it’s easy to keep answering messages or watching videos long after we intended to go to bed. Some people also **prioritise productivity over rest**, as though an extra hour awake automatically means an extra hour of useful work. In reality, those habits can **cut into sleep time** without producing much benefit. Staying up to finish a task may occasionally be necessary, but doing it every night is often **a false economy** if you’re exhausted the following day.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **long working hours** — uzoq ish soatlari
2. **constant digital stimulation** — raqamli qurilmalardan tinimsiz qo‘zg‘alish/ta’sir
3. **prioritise productivity over rest** — unumdorlikni dam olishdan ustun qo‘yish
4. **cut into sleep time** — uyqu vaqtini qisqartirib yuborish
5. **a false economy** — foydadek ko‘rinsa-da, oxir-oqibat zarar keltiradigan yo‘l

### 2. Should schools and workplaces change their schedules to help people get enough sleep? Why or why not?

**Sizning original javobingiz**

> In my opinion, the first people should change their thoughts about sleep, and schools and workplaces should change their schedule to help people get enough sleep. Why? You know, many schools and workplaces set up their schedules at night, even at night. And if someone should earn money, he tries, or people try to work even at night, and it affects their mental health and health. And so they couldn't get enough sleep. But if the schools and workplaces change their schedules, they can sleep better. So that's it.

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer — taxminan 35–40 soniya**

In some cases, yes. For example, **early start times** can be difficult for students who travel a long way, while people who **work irregular shifts** may struggle to maintain a consistent routine. Where practical, **flexible working hours** could give people more control over their mornings. Schools and employers should also look at whether workloads leave enough time to **protect sleep quality**. Of course, hospitals and other essential services cannot simply close at night, so the goal is to **find a workable balance** rather than impose one timetable on everyone.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **early start times** — erta boshlanadigan dars yoki ish vaqti
2. **work irregular shifts** — notekis smenalarda ishlash
3. **flexible working hours** — moslashuvchan ish soatlari
4. **protect sleep quality** — uyqu sifatini asrash
5. **find a workable balance** — amalda ishlaydigan muvozanatni topish

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer — taxminan 35–40 soniya**

It can do either, depending on how people use it. A watch may help someone **track health metrics** or provide **reminders to move** during a long day at a desk. On the other hand, streaming and social media can encourage **sedentary habits**, especially if they replace exercise or sleep. I wouldn’t call technology itself healthy or unhealthy. The important thing is to **use technology mindfully**: let it support your goals without letting it dominate your free time. For me, that distinction is what **tips the balance**.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **track health metrics** — salomatlik ko‘rsatkichlarini kuzatish
2. **reminders to move** — harakat qilishni eslatuvchi bildirishnomalar

### 4. Who should take more responsibility for people’s health: individuals or governments?

**Sizning original javobingiz**

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer — taxminan 35–40 soniya**

I’d say responsibility is shared. Individuals have to **take personal responsibility** for their daily routines and **make informed choices** about their habits. But governments can **create supportive environments**, for example by ensuring that neighbourhoods have places where people can be active and **reliable health information** is easy to find. People’s options also depend on their income and working hours, so telling everyone simply to “try harder” overlooks real constraints. In my view, public policy should remove unnecessary barriers, while individuals **set sensible boundaries** and make use of the opportunities available to them.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **take personal responsibility** — shaxsiy mas’uliyatni o‘z zimmasiga olish
2. **make informed choices** — yetarli ma’lumotga asoslangan tanlov qilish

4. **reliable health information** — ishonchli salomatlik ma’lumotlari
5. **set sensible boundaries** — oqilona chegaralar belgilash

**Sizning original javobingiz**

**Band 9 uslubidagi sample answer — taxminan 35–40 soniya**

Governments could begin with **public awareness campaigns** that explain practical habits in clear language. They could also invest in **safe public spaces**, such as parks and walking routes, so that being active is convenient rather than expensive. Schools and public institutions can **lead by example** by making room for movement during the day. I think the aim should be to **make healthy choices accessible**, not force everyone into the same routine. That approach is more likely to support **long-term behaviour change** because people can adapt the advice to their own circumstances.

**Ishlatilgan 5 ta collocation**

1. **public awareness campaigns** — ommaviy xabardorlik kampaniyalari
2. **safe public spaces** — xavfsiz jamoat joylari
3. **lead by example** — shaxsiy namuna ko‘rsatish
4. **make healthy choices accessible** — sog‘lom tanlovlarni hamma uchun qulay qilish
5. **long-term behaviour change** — uzoq muddatli xulq-atvor o‘zgarishi
